Privacy Policy

Applies to Rally Clock, Checkaboo and other apps published by NitroCat Innovations, LLC.
Effective November 13, 2026.

The short version: our apps keep your data on your device. We don't have accounts, we don't run servers that receive your data, and we don't use analytics, advertising, or tracking of any kind. We never sell or share your data. The one thing to know is that on Android, Checkaboo's QR scanner uses Google's ML Kit, which sends Google basic technical diagnostics; see Checkaboo, below.

Rally Clock

What the app stores

Rally Clock saves the information you enter — event setup, car numbers, times, comments, and your settings — in the app's private storage on your device. It stays there until you delete it or uninstall the app.

Location

Rally Clock uses your device's location only for these features:

  • Satellite time check (Android only) — one reading, used only for its time. The app takes one when it starts, if you have already allowed location, and whenever you tap for it.
  • Trip meters — while a trip meter is running, the app reads your location to measure distance, including with the screen locked. On Android a notification shows whenever this is happening, and on iPhone and iPad the location indicator does. It stops when you stop the trip meter.

Location is processed on your device. Your position is never stored, never included in a log, and never sent to us or to anyone else.

When data leaves your device

Only when you act, and only to places you choose:

  • Sharing or handing over a log — to another device running Rally Clock on the same local network, or through your phone's share menu (email, messaging, files, and so on). A log includes your name and phone number if you entered them in Setup. Hand-overs go directly between devices on the local network and are not encrypted, so use a network you trust. Once shared, that copy is handled by the recipient and the app you shared it with.
  • Loading an event setup from another device on your local network.
  • Finding other devices — while a device is sharing its clock, collecting logs, or handing out a setup, it announces its control name and the event name on the local network so other devices can find it.
  • Setting the clock — the app asks a public internet time server (by default pool.ntp.org, changeable in Settings) for the current time. Like any internet request, this reveals your device's IP address to that server. No other data is sent.

Checkaboo

Checkaboo is a checklist app. It has no accounts, no servers of ours, and it does not ask for your location.

What the app stores

Checkaboo saves your checklists and your answers, with any notes you type, in the app's private storage on your device. It stays there until you delete it or uninstall the app.

Camera

The camera is used for one thing: scanning a checklist's QR code that another phone is showing. The picture is read on your device and thrown away. Nothing from the camera is saved or sent by us.

On Android, reading the QR code is done by Google's ML Kit, built into the app. ML Kit does the reading on your device. Google documents that ML Kit also sends Google basic technical diagnostics: your device's maker, model and Android version, the app's name and version, an identifier for this installation of the app, and performance figures such as how long a scan took and any error codes. It does not send the camera picture, the QR code's contents, or your checklists. Google describes this data on its ML Kit data disclosure page. We do not receive it. On iPhone, iPad and Mac the app uses Apple's own scanner and sends nothing.

When data leaves your device

Only when you act, and only to places you choose:

  • Importing from a Google Sheets link — the app fetches that one sheet from Google. Like any internet request, this reveals your device's IP address to Google, along with the sheet's address. The sheet is only downloaded; nothing is uploaded.
  • Sharing a checklist — as a QR code someone scans, as a code you copy, or as a file through your device's share menu (email, messaging, files, and so on). A shared checklist contains its steps and tasks, including any details you wrote for them, but none of your answers or notes from a run. Once shared, that copy is handled by the recipient and the app you shared it with.

Opening a spreadsheet from your device's files reads that file on your device only.

Permissions

Checkaboo asks for internet access (to fetch a Google Sheets link you give it) and the camera (to scan a QR code). It asks for the camera only when you choose to scan, and you can revoke it at any time in your device settings; everything else keeps working without it. It never asks for your location, contacts, photos or microphone.

What we collect

Nothing. NitroCat Innovations, LLC does not receive, collect, or store any personal information from our apps. The only personal information that leaves your device is what you send yourself, such as your name and phone number in a log you hand over to event staff. If you email us, we use your message and address only to reply. The one related thing outside our control is the Android QR scanner in Checkaboo, described above, whose diagnostics go to Google and not to us.

Permissions in Rally Clock

Location, local network access, and notifications are requested only when a feature needs them, and you can revoke them at any time in your device settings. The rest of the app keeps working without them.

Deleting your data

Because your data lives only on your device, you control it completely: delete logs within the app, clear the app's data in your device settings, or uninstall the app. There is no account to close.

Children

Our apps are not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and the effective date above. If a future version of an app collects data in a new way, this policy will say so before that version is released.
